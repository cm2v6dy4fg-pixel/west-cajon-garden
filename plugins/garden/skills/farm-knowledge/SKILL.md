# West Cajon farm knowledge

The garden calendar in this repo sits on a 5-acre high-desert farm. This skill holds what has been
learned about that farm: the site, the owner's decisions, the rules every design must respect, and a
library of methods filtered through this site. Read `references/00-site-lens.md` before any design,
land-management or business answer; open the other references only when the question needs them.

## The site in brief

- 5 acres in Phelan, CA, about 4,330-4,360 ft. Mojave / high-desert transition (Joshua tree, juniper,
  creosote scrub). Alkaline sandy alluvium assumed until a soil test says otherwise. Fenced field about 181 x 100 m.
  A ~50 ha wash crosses the property.
- Weather comes from the West Cajon station (`data/station.json`). Its first year (Oct 2025-Sep 2026)
  was a wet El Niño year: 24 in of rain, ~21 in of it Nov-Feb, 0.6 in Mar-Aug, one storm of 8.4 in in 22 hours;
  10 frost nights, coldest 26 °F, max 98 °F, ~873-924 chill hours, ~3,200 GDD (base 50 °F).
  Long-term normals are far drier (roughly 6-14 in, unverified): size storage for a dry year of 6-8 in.
- Winds: S/SW gusts most of the year, NE Santa Anas in fall.
- Water is hauled at **$60 per 1,000 gal**. The existing well is weak (a 7 GPM pump over-pumps it) and its yield
  has never been tested.
- Plan documents (kept in the owner's project files, not this repo): the Sep 27 2026 Keyline Plant Plan
  (latest; supersedes V7), the keyline_design_v3 map/KML, the Master Guide, Action Plan and Action Packet.
  Mature water demand in the plan is ~211-231k gal/yr; primary production nets about $17.6-31.6k/yr at maturity.

## The owner's decisions (2026-10-11; these override older plan documents)

1. **Goal:** self-sufficiency and financial independence. The farm feeds the household first (groceries not bought
   count as income) and "independent" means the farm covers the household's current bills. The older
   "$150k+/yr income replacement" goal in the Master Guide is retired.
2. **Time:** 4-16 hours a week beside a day job. Automate first (drip on timers, deep mulch, deep-litter pens) and
   start with low-hours, flexible-timing income: eggs, pig shares, nursery stock from cuttings.
3. **Pond:** a regeneration pond, built as an unlined recharge and habitat basin that fills in storms and soaks in.
   It need not hold water all year. Water to be pumped later goes in covered tanks.
4. **Pigs:** at most 9 on the farm at a time, as the starting point: two batches a year finishing spring and fall
   (16-18 pigs/yr) on KNF deep litter. Scale only if batches pay and the litter system keeps up.

## Ten rules every answer must respect

1. **Plan in Regrarians order:** climate (including the holistic context above), geography, water, access,
   ecosystems, buildings, fences, soils, economy, energy.
2. **Water is the binding constraint and arrives in pulses.** Design for the 8-inch storm and the six dry months at
   once. Plan every overflow as a resource.
3. **Rank enterprises by net $ per 1,000 gal and per labour hour.** Anything netting under ~$60/1,000 gal loses
   money on hauled water. Plan figures: pork ~$621, nursery ~$393, jujube ~$53, pomegranate ~$50, stone fruit
   $9-13 per 1,000 gal.
4. **Desert-adapt keyline.** Few swales; keyline pattern cultivation, many small basins and half-moons sized with
   C:CA = (CWR − DR) / (DR × K × Eff), armored spillways, off-channel storage. Passive treatments (cover, litter,
   animal impact) before earthworks.
5. **Open water evaporates** (~2 m/yr; the plan's own pond loses more than its volume each year). Store water in
   soil and covered tanks; ponds are recharge.
6. **Biome model: desert wash / mesquite bosque with a pinyon-juniper edge,** not oak savanna. Main crops by water
   return: jujube, pomegranate, pistachio, grape, fig; stone fruit at home scale only. Plant mass from seed and
   cuttings (STUN) to find adapted genetics and stock the nursery.
7. **Windbreaks, shade and wood-chip mulch come before any soil input.**
8. **Biology needs moisture.** KNF/JADAM inputs are cheap but thinly evidenced; apply them only where water already
   goes. The strongest fit is the inoculated deep-litter pig and poultry pen.
9. **Revegetation:** vertical mulch, rock structures and protected transplants first; seed balls only as a cheap
   extra just before winter rain.
10. **Income realism:** primary production will not cover everything. Cash grows from nursery stock,
    water-harvesting design-install work, education and value-added products, with the farm as the demonstration.

Two tests for any new project: does it cut a household cost, or bring in more cash per 1,000 gal and per hour
than what it replaces? If neither, defer it. Does it fit the 4-16 hour week?

## Working rules

- Regulations and fees were researched in October 2026. Say so, and tell the owner to confirm with the agency
  before spending money.
- Keep personal details (address, parcel numbers, finances, contracts) out of this repo; it is public.
- When a calculation matters, show it. `python3 "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/farm_math.py"` does the water
  cost, per-gallon return, roof/land yield and C:CA checks.
- Planting dates belong in `crops.json` (see the garden-calendar skill); this skill covers the farm around it.
