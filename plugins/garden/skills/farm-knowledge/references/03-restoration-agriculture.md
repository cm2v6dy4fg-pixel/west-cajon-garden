# Restoration Agriculture (Mark Shepard)

Mark Shepard, *Restoration Agriculture: Real-World Permaculture for Farmers* (Acres U.S.A., 2013); also *Water for
Any Farm* (2018), the most accessible modern keyline book. Shepard runs New Forest Farm (~106 acres, Wisconsin),
an oak-savanna mimic since 1995. Influences: J. Russell Smith (*Tree Crops*, 1929), Fukuoka, Mollison, Yeomans.

## Core argument
- Annual agriculture destroys topsoil; staple-annual civilisations collapse.
- The savanna (trees with open grass between) is the most productive terrestrial biome for consumers. Farm in its
  image: trees, shrubs, canes, vines, forage, fungi and livestock together.
- Perennial planting cost is paid once and amortised; cost of production falls every year.

## The steps (ch. 7)
1. **Identify your biome:** plant and animal community, rainfall pattern, soils, successional pathway.
2. **Find the key economic species:** the dominant trees and their useful relatives.
3. **Imitate the system:** same structure (tall trees, shrubs, canes, vines, ground layer, fungi, animals) with
   productive species from the same families.

## Water first (ch. 13)
- Water management is "the very first step," using Keyline; the sweet spot is nearly always on or near a keyline.
- Keyline swales and berms with pocket ponds at valley crossings; the pattern sets all woody rows, alleys parallel.
- Keyline subsoiling yearly; design for the maximum regional storm.

## Transitional strategy (ch. 14)
- Tree rows on keyline with alleys sized to your equipment; keep annual crops or grazing in alleys while trees
  establish, then shift to perennial and livestock yields.
- Weed control in the tree row matters most in years 1-5: wood chips or sawdust are best.

## STUN: Sheer, Total, Utter Neglect
- Plant many (ideally from seed), care minimally, let survivors reveal adapted genetics; propagate the survivors.
- "Restoration agriculture farmers ask what can I not do and get away with it."
- Balance: some weed control and water during establishment (2-5 years), but no input dependency.

## Livestock and bees (ch. 9-10)
- Rotate species through alleys; animals clean up dropped and infested fruit and cycle fertility.
- Goats strip bark; guard trees or keep them out. Bees are an integral yield.

## Making a profit (ch. 17)
- ~80% of US Schedule F farmers earn most income off-farm: "your agricultural enterprise probably will not pay all
  of your bills. Don't beat yourself up over this."
- Beware the conference "myth of profitable agriculture" (high tunnels, aquaponics, expensive equipment); he names
  fish-plus-basil systems specifically.
- Lower cost of production per unit; perennials amortise planting cost; adapted, unsprayed plants cut cost again.
- Sell value-added and direct, diversify, and never spend $40 of inputs to grow $20 of fruit.

## Translating Shepard to the Mojave high desert
Biome analogs on and near the site: desert wash / mesquite bosque, pinyon-juniper woodland, Joshua tree woodland,
saltbush scrub.

| Savanna layer | Phelan analog (drought-proven) |
|---|---|
| Tall nut tree | Pistachio (Kerman/Peters on UCB-1), pinyon pine (*Pinus monophylla*, very slow), almond (late-blooming; frost risk), carob (frost-tender below ~20 °F) |
| Medium tree | Honey mesquite (pods for flour and feed, N-fixing, bee forage), jujube, pomegranate (tree form), olive, apricot/apple at home scale, Afghan/Eldarica pine (windbreak) |
| Shrub | Pomegranate (bush), blue elderberry, goji, fourwing saltbush (forage), Apache plume, desert willow, sumac (*Rhus trilobata*) |
| Cane/vine | Grape (best water return), fig (protected spots), blackberry only with drip |
| Ground | Asparagus, prickly pear (fruit, pads, forage), native bunchgrasses, rain-window cover crops, melons and squash |
| Fungi | Limited in dry sand; possible in shaded, irrigated wood-chip beds (wine cap) |
| Animals | Pigs (deep litter + alley rotation), poultry, bees, possibly sheep in the alleys |

**Variety evidence:** University of Nevada, Reno *Fruit Trees for Southern Nevada 2024-25* (preliminary, no
statistics): 'Brown Turkey' fig top yielder (79 lb/tree); 'Black Jack' fig, 'July Elberta', 'May Pride' and
'Stark Saturn' peach, and 'Desert Delight' nectarine did well; 'Panache' and 'Kadota' fig and 'Babcock' peach did
poorly. Las Vegas is hotter and lower than Phelan: use it for heat tolerance and keep checking chill hours.

**STUN here:** plant mesquite, jujube, pomegranate, grape cuttings and pistachio rootstock seed in quantity in the
rain window with minimal irrigation; propagate the survivors for the nursery business. The plan's nursery
(grape, pomegranate and elderberry cuttings; mesquite and saltbush seed) already does this.
