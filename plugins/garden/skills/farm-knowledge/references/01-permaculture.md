# Permaculture

## Origins and core texts
- Coined by Bill Mollison and David Holmgren in Tasmania in the 1970s (*Permaculture One*, 1978).
- *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* (Mollison, 1988) is the reference; chapter 11 "Dryland Strategies" is the
  one that matters here.
- *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* (Holmgren, 2002): the 12 principles below.
- Free open textbook: *Introduction to Permaculture* (Oregon State / Andrew Millison), open.oregonstate.education/permaculture.
- Worth owning: Hemenway *Gaia's Garden*; Jacke & Toensmeier *Edible Forest Gardens*; Falk *The Resilient Farm and
  Homestead*; Holzer *Desert or Paradise*; Toensmeier *The Carbon Farming Solution*; Brad Lancaster *Rainwater
  Harvesting for Drylands and Beyond* vol 1-3 (the dryland bible).

## Ethics
Earth care, people care, fair share (return of surplus).

## Holmgren's 12 principles
1. Observe and interact
2. Catch and store energy
3. Obtain a yield
4. Apply self-regulation and accept feedback
5. Use and value renewable resources and services
6. Produce no waste
7. Design from patterns to details
8. Integrate rather than segregate
9. Use small and slow solutions
10. Use and value diversity
11. Use edges and value the marginal
12. Creatively use and respond to change

## Mollison's working principles
- **Relative location:** place each element where it connects usefully to others (tanks above the garden, chickens
  between orchard and compost).
- **Each element performs many functions; each important function is supported by many elements** (water from
  roof + basin + well + soil storage).
- Energy-efficient planning (zones, sectors, slope); use biological resources instead of machines and inputs;
  energy cycling; small-scale intensive systems; accelerating succession; diversity; edge effect.
- "The problem is the solution"; "work with nature, not against it"; "everything gardens."

## Design tools
- **Zones 0-5** by frequency of visit: 0 house; 1 kitchen garden, nursery, herbs (daily); 2 orchard, chickens,
  compost; 3 main crops, larger livestock; 4 managed semi-wild, forage, woodlot; 5 wild, observation only.
- **Sectors:** sun angles, prevailing and storm winds, fire (high-desert brush fire comes with wind), flood (the
  wash), views, noise, cold-air drainage, wildlife.
- **Scale of permanence** (Yeomans): climate, landform, water, access, trees, buildings, fences, soil. Doherty
  expanded it into the Regrarians Platform (`02-keyline-regrarians.md`).
- Design process: SADIMET (Survey, Analysis, Design, Implementation, Maintenance, Evaluation, Tweaking).
- **Guilds** around a central tree (nitrogen fixer, dynamic accumulator, insectary, groundcover); **chop and drop**.

## Dryland strategies (Mollison ch. 11, Lancaster, Lawton)
- Harvest water at the top; store it in the soil first, then tanks, then ponds.
- Earth-shaping: basins, net-and-pan, boomerang/half-moon berms, check dams, gabions, imprinting, Zuni waffle
  gardens, **sunken (not raised) beds** to collect water and cut wind.
- **Pioneer support species first:** nitrogen-fixing, drought-hardy trees and shrubs (honey/velvet mesquite, palo
  verde, acacia, desert willow, saltbush, locust) ahead of fruit trees for shade, wind and fertility.
- Shade and windbreak before production; microclimates on the north side of berms and buildings.
- Mulch everything (wood chips beat straw in wind; rock mulch around trunks).
- Drip, ollas, deep pipes, wicking; water deeply and less often.
- Greywater to trees (California laundry-to-landscape needs no permit if it meets CPC Ch. 15).
- Plant in the rain window: sow and transplant Nov-Feb.

## Dryland practitioners
Geoff Lawton (Greening the Desert, Jordan); Brad Lancaster (Tucson; eight principles in `08-water-harvesting.md`);
Neal Spackman (Al Baydha, Saudi Arabia: community and grazing control mattered as much as earthworks); Andrew
Millison (desert water harvesting worldwide); Craig Sponholtz and Bill Zeedyk; Watershed Management Group ("slow
it, spread it, sink it").

## How it applies here
- Zone 1: the house-garage-tank cluster at the high SW corner (roof water by gravity). Zone 2: bench row, nursery
  pad, hive yard. Zone 3: keyline alleys and pig cells. Zone 4: windbreak bands. Zone 5: the wash buffer.
- The plan is a good permaculture design but leans on swales; re-check each against "start small, at the top, many
  small basins" and Doherty's swale critique.
- Biggest levers not yet in the plan: greywater to the nearest trees, and sunken beds for annuals.
