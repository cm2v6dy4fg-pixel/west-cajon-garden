# The site lens: how every school applies to the West Cajon farm

Every other reference here is general; this one filters them through the actual site so design,
land-management and business work starts from the right constraints. Site facts and the owner's decisions
are in `../SKILL.md`.

## The ten design rules, with the reasoning

1. **Water is the binding constraint, and it arrives in pulses.** Design for the storm (8 in in a day) and the
   drought (6 dry months) at the same time. Lancaster's "plan the overflow as a resource" and Yeomans' "water
   before roads, trees and buildings" are the two rules that matter most. See `08-water-harvesting.md`.
2. **Judge every enterprise in dollars per 1,000 gallons.** At $60/1,000 gal hauled, anything netting under
   ~$60/1,000 gal loses money if it runs on hauled water. Plan figures: pork ~$621, nursery ~$393, jujube ~$53,
   pomegranate ~$50, stone fruit $9-13 per 1,000 gal.
3. **Groundwater is adjudicated, and the well is unproven.** Phelan sits in the Mojave Water Agency area. Under
   10 acre-feet/yr is "Minimal Producer" status (see `compliance.md`). Mature demand (~231,000 gal) is only ~0.7 AF,
   so a productive well would change the economics, but the existing well is weak. **Testing the well's yield is
   the highest-value single action on the property.** Until then, plan on hauled water.
4. **Keyline and swales need desert adaptation.** Doherty calls swales "largely redundant" where keyline pattern
   cultivation (KPC) is done properly and prefers passive treatments first. On sandy desert soil with flash floods,
   prefer (a) many small basins, half-moons and microcatchments sized by the catchment-to-cultivated-area ratio;
   (b) armored spillways on every structure; (c) off-channel storage fed from the wash; (d) KPC only where soil
   moisture and cover can hold the benefit. See `02-keyline-regrarians.md`.
5. **Pick the biome model before the species.** The analog is a desert wash / mesquite bosque and pinyon-juniper
   woodland edge: honey mesquite, desert willow, blue elderberry, pinyon, juniper, saltbush, Apache plume, with
   drought-proven crops (jujube, pomegranate, pistachio, grape, fig, olive, almond on the right rootstock, Afghan
   pine). See `03-restoration-agriculture.md`.
6. **Shade, wind and mulch beat fertility.** Moisture, heat load and wind desiccation limit plants here before NPK
   does. Windbreaks on the S/SW, shade for establishment, thick wood-chip mulch, then soil inputs. See
   `06-agroforestry.md`.
7. **Soil biology needs moisture to exist.** KNF, compost teas and inoculants do little in dry sand; apply them in
   basins, drip zones, deep-litter pens and compost. The KNF deep-litter piggery (CTAHR LM-23) is the strongest fit
   because it solves odor, flies and manure with bedding the pigs keep moist. See `04-knf-jadam.md`.
8. **Animals carry the early economics, trees the long-term value.** Pork, eggs, honey and nursery stock produce
   cash in years 1-3; trees and vines take 4-10 years. Stack enterprises and keep costs of production low.
9. **Primary production will not cover everything.** It nets roughly $17-32k/yr at maturity. The farm's goal is to
   cover household bills, so add high-value-per-gallon lines that fit the time budget: nursery and native plants,
   design-install services for neighbours (keyline and water harvesting), education, value-added products. Treat the
   farm as a demonstration site. See `10-business.md`.
10. **Regulation shapes the business.** County animal limits, apiary registration, custom-exempt meat sales,
    cottage food classes, nursery licensing. See `compliance.md`.

## How each school maps onto this site

| School | Use it here for | Be careful about |
|---|---|---|
| Regrarians / Keyline (Doherty, Yeomans) | Planning order; holistic context; keypoint/keyline analysis of the wash; storage; KPC | Keyline was built for 25-35 in rainfall in NSW; here it is water-spreading and storage logic, not pasture irrigation |
| Restoration Agriculture (Shepard) | Biome mimicry, alley rows on keyline, STUN selection from seed, livestock in alleys, cost-of-production thinking | His oak savanna list does not fit; chestnut and hazel are poor fits |
| Permaculture (Mollison, Holmgren, Lancaster, Lawton) | Zones and sectors, dryland strategies, water harvesting, stacking functions, greywater | Food-forest density does not survive 6 months of drought without irrigation |
| Korean Natural Farming / JADAM | Deep-litter pig and poultry pens, compost inoculation, cheap liquid inputs in drip and nursery | Thin evidence; IMO quality varies; inputs only work with moisture |
| Fukuoka natural farming | Seed balls before winter rain, no-till, "how about not doing this?" | His system relied on ~1,600 mm of rain; desert seed-ball results are poor |
| Agroforestry (USDA, syntropic) | Windbreaks (NRCS 380), alley cropping (311), silvopasture (381), hedgerows (422), tree establishment (612) with cost-share | Syntropic density needs water; only in irrigated zones |
| Regenerative ag / soil health | Cover, living roots, minimal disturbance, diversity, animals; compost and mulch | Cover crops only grow in the Nov-Mar rain window |
| Holistic Management (Savory) | Holistic context, financial planning (plan profit first), testing decisions | Planned-grazing claims are contested; on 5 desert acres grazing is mostly feeding plus impact |

## Funding that fits this site

- **NRCS EQIP** (Victorville service center): cost-share for windbreaks, tree/shrub establishment, micro-irrigation,
  water harvesting, roof runoff structures, high tunnels, mulching, pollinator habitat. Beginning farmers get higher
  rates and an advance-payment option. Contracts must be signed before any work starts.
- **CDFA Healthy Soils Program**: compost, mulching, hedgerows, windbreaks, cover crops. Not open to individual
  farmers in 2026; producer funding expected in 2027 through partner organizations.
- **USDA FSA** microloans and beginning farmer loans for equipment, livestock and nursery setup.
- **Mojave Desert Resource Conservation District**: local technical help, native seed and windbreak stock.
- **Western SARE** farmer grants for on-farm trials; **REAP** for solar pumping; Wildlife Conservation Board Desert
  Conservation Program for larger habitat projects.

## Open questions that block better design

1. Well yield (drawdown and recovery test), well completion report, water quality (EC, sodium, boron, pH,
   bicarbonate, TDS, nitrate, arsenic, fluoride).
2. Soil test: texture, pH, EC, SAR, caliche depth, organic matter. Percolation test at the recharge basin site.
3. Long-term rainfall normals for the parcel (NOAA / PRISM) to replace the one wet year.
4. Which income lines the owner wants to run (people-facing vs solitary work).
