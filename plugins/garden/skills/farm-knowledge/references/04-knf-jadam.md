# Korean Natural Farming (KNF) and JADAM

## Who and what
- **Cho Han Kyu** (Janong Natural Farming Institute, Korea) developed KNF from the 1960s. English texts: *Cho's
  Global Natural Farming* (SARRA, 2010); *Natural Farming: Agriculture Materials* (Cho Ju Young).
- **Cho Global Natural Farming** spread it to Hawai'i (Hoon Park, Drew Toher, Chris Trump), the Philippines and beyond.
- **JADAM** (Youngsang Cho, 1991) is a simplified, ultra-low-cost offshoot: *JADAM Organic Farming* (free English
  PDF via UNDP IDEASS); JADAM YouTube channel.
- Philosophy: indigenous microorganisms (IMO) and local materials; feed the soil food web; follow the crop's
  nutritive cycle (N-rich inputs in vegetative growth, P/Ca at transition and fruiting); no tillage; deep-litter
  animal housing.

## Input recipes (University of Hawai'i CTAHR fact sheets)
All inputs: food-grade glass or PE, breathable cloth cover, no metal, out of light and heat, loose lids.

| Input | Recipe (short) | Dilution | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IMO 1-4** (BIO-9) | (1) Cedar box of steamed rice under leaf litter in a healthy local woodland 3-5 days, collect white mould; (2) mix with equal weight brown sugar, 7 days; (3) dilute 1:500 into wheat mill run at 65-70% moisture, pile, keep ≤50 °C/122 °F, turn 3-4 times over 7 days; (4) mix 1:1 with field soil | Topdress, add to compost | Inoculate soil and compost |
| **FPJ** fermented plant juice (SA-7) | Fast-growing shoot tips before sunrise, unwashed, 1:1 brown sugar by weight, pack 2/3 full, 3-7 days | 1:500 (1:800-1:1,000 in heat) | Weekly foliar/drench, late afternoon |
| **LAB** lactic acid bacteria (SA-8) | Rice-rinse water 3-5 days → 1 part to 10 parts milk → 3-5 days → keep the yellow whey | 1:1,000 | Foliar, seed, compost, livestock water, deep-litter odour |
| **OHN** oriental herbal nutrient (SA-11) | Angelica 2 : licorice 1 : cinnamon 1 : ginger 1 : garlic 1, fermented with brown sugar, extracted in 40% alcohol over 14-day cycles | 1:1,000 (1:1,500 in drought) | Stress resilience, mildew, livestock water |
| **DSW** diluted seawater (SA-9) | Seawater rested 24 h | 1:30 | Minerals; skip on alkaline/saline desert soil |
| **WCA** water-soluble calcium (SA-10) | Roasted eggshells in brown rice vinegar 1:10 by weight until bubbling stops (7-10 days) | 1:1,000 | Flowering and fruit set |
| **FAA** fish amino acid (SA-12) | Fish waste + equal weight brown sugar, with IMO4 and a little OHN, 2-6 months | 1:1,000 | Nitrogen, vegetative stage only |
| **WCP** water-soluble calcium phosphate | Charred bones in BRV | 1:1,000 | Transition to flowering |
| **BRV** brown rice vinegar | Purchased or made | 1:500-1:1,000 | Carrier |
| **IDLS piggery** (LM-23, DuPonte & Fischer 2012) | Inoculated deep litter: IMO + green waste/wood chips, natural ventilation, roof oriented for sun, dry enough to ferment | n/a | No washing, little odour, no fly breeding, litter becomes compost |

## JADAM core inputs
- **JMS** (microbial solution): leaf mould + boiled potato + rainwater + a pinch of sea salt, 1-3 days. A published
  home recipe: 1 boiled potato and 1 handful of leaf mould in separate cotton socks in a bucket of non-chlorinated
  water with ½ tbsp sea salt, knead, cover, use when surface foam peaks (1-5 days; only above ~4 °C). Dilute 1:10-1:50.
- **JLF** (liquid fertiliser): any plant or animal material in water with a handful of leaf mould, sealed for months.
- **JWA** (wetting agent): home soap from vegetable oil and potassium hydroxide; spreader and contact pesticide.
- **JS** (sulfur) and **JHS** (herbal solutions) for pests and disease.
- Differences from KNF: no brown sugar, fewer and cheaper inputs, larger volumes, soil as the inoculant source.
- Practical English guide: Nigel Palmer, *The Regenerative Grower's Guide to Garden Amendments*.

## Evidence
- CTAHR sheets are practical guides, not trials; the IMO bulletin calls its practices "suggested, rather than
  recommended."
- Keli'ikuli (2018, UH thesis): higher bacterial diversity in KNF plots; a second IMO dose at 14 days raised yield;
  n = 3, suggestive only.
- SARE FNE22-001 (NY, 2022): IMO cost far more per area than compost (~$46-122 vs ~$10 per 100 sq ft at 1 in), was
  low in OM and N, bacteria-dominated, and went anaerobic when wet. The farm chose compost.
- Thompson et al. (2025 preprint): IMO is a selective enrichment dominated by a few taxa, not a copy of the forest
  soil community.
- Swine deep litter has the most practical support (Hawai'i adoption, odour and fly control).
- Takeaway: cheap, safe, useful as compost accelerators, nursery and drip biostimulants, and for deep-litter
  housing; not proven to replace compost or minerals.

## How it applies here
1. **Deep-litter pig pens (highest value):** roofed sleeping and feeding area facing south for winter sun,
   cross-ventilation, 2-3 ft of chips/straw/green waste with IMO, LAB for odour. Litter composts back into the
   tree rows.
2. **IMO source:** no moist forest litter on site. Collect under the oldest shrubs (mature elderberry, mesquite) or
   in pinyon-juniper woodland in the San Gabriel foothills, Dec-Mar. Expect sparse growth; keep moisture moderate.
3. **Only where water already goes:** nursery pots, drip emitters, greenhouse beds, compost, basins.
4. **Cheapest inputs:** LAB (milk + rice water), FPJ from spring weeds (mallow, purslane, mugwort, Russian thistle
   tips), WCA from the flock's eggshells, JLF from weeds, FAA if fish scraps are available. Skip OHN early.
5. **Skip DSW and salt;** test soil EC and SAR first.
6. **Poultry:** KNF deep-litter coop with IMO and LAB in water.
