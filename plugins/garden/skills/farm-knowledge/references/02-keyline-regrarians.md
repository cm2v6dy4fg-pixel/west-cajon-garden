# Regrarians Platform, Keyline and Darren J. Doherty

## Who
- **P.A. Yeomans** (1905-1984), Australian engineer; developed Keyline on Nevallan and Yobarnie near Sydney. Books:
  *The Keyline Plan* (1954), *The Challenge of Landscape* (1958, free PDF from Yeomans Plow Co), *Water for Every
  Farm* (1965), *The City Forest* (1971). Invented the Yeomans shank subsoiler. Ken Yeomans runs Keyline Designs;
  Allan Yeomans runs Yeomans Plow Co.
- **Darren J. Doherty** founded Regrarians Ltd and the Regrarians Platform (2006), a broadening of Yeomans' scale
  of permanence. Runs REX training, open consultancies, REX Online and the *Regrarians Handbook* (chapters 1-7
  released; Soils, Economy, Energy in progress as of 2026).

## Yeomans' Keyline Scale of Permanence (1958)
Climate → land shape → water supply → roads → trees → buildings → subdivision (fences) → soil. Plan from the most
permanent to the least; each layer serves the ones above it.

## The Regrarians Platform: 10 layers, in order
1. **Climate:** human climate (holistic context: decision makers, purpose, quality of life, future resource base,
   risk, regulation) and biospheric climate (sun, temperature, GDD, chill, rain, ET, frost, wind).
2. **Geography:** landform, keyline analysis (primary valleys, ridges, keypoints), geology, neighbours, utilities.
3. **Water:** "the principal planning medium": catchment, harvesting, storage, reticulation, irrigation, riparian
   restoration.
4. **Access:** roads and lanes placed to serve water (on ridges or a grade, as harvesting surfaces, not on contour).
5. **Ecosystems:** flora, fauna, fungi, livestock, crops and forestry placed to support other systems.
6. **Buildings.**
7. **Fences:** follow the permanent layers; use flexible (electric) fencing.
8. **Soils:** easily destroyed, easily rebuilt; cheapest effective treatments.
9. **Economy:** markets, terms of trade, compliance, capital flows that drive regeneration.
10. **Energy:** enhance photosynthesis first, then generation and saving.

The Regrarians Platform Checklist V8 is the intake form for layers 1-5 (a copy is in the owner's project files).

## Keyline concepts
- **Primary valley:** the smallest valley that carries water; the unit Keyline works within.
- **Keypoint:** where a primary valley floor changes from convex (steeper) to concave (flatter). Water slows and soil
  deepens; traditional site for the keypoint dam.
- **Keyline:** the contour through the keypoint, extended across the valley.
- **Keyline pattern cultivation (KPC):** narrow-tine subsoiling parallel to the keyline. Below the keyline the rips
  fall gently toward the ridges, drifting runoff from wet valleys to dry ridges.
- **Progressive deepening:** start shallow and deepen over seasons as roots follow; fall is generally best; the
  principle matters more than the tool.
- **Dam hierarchy:** ridge dams, keypoint dams, contour/saddle dams, lower valley dams, filled by compacted channels
  on a slight gradient.
- **Keyline geographic analysis:** find ridges, primary valleys and keypoints first; then roads on ridges, dams in
  valleys and at keypoints, trees along keylines and ridges, fences following land units.

## Doherty's positions (permies.com Q&A)
- **Swales:** "The swale is largely redundant if for example you are using KPC properly." Absorbent ditches are a
  suboptimal way to move water to storage; compacted channels on a gradient are the Keyline tool.
- **Passive first:** question any earthwork before passive treatments such as ground cover, litter and high-density,
  short-duration animal impact.
- **Cost:** KPC about $0.02/m² vs about $0.75/m² for an equivalent swale system (his estimate); KPC is not permanent.
- **Effects vs causes:** swales and KPC often treat runoff and erosion rather than bare soil and compaction.
- Swales built from dispersive clays fail quickly; roads do not belong on contour.

## Regrarians soil sequence (paraphrase, not a quote)
(1) Stop the damage (cover, rest, grazing plan); (2) KPC/subsoiling; (3) biological stimulants; (4) targeted
minerals from soil tests; (5) diverse cover and planned grazing; (6) monitor (infiltration, ground cover, brix).

## How it applies here
- Layer 1 is now set: self-sufficiency, farm covers household bills, 4-16 hours a week (see `../SKILL.md`).
- The wash is a primary valley; the plan's keypoint and off-channel basin with a rock take-off sill is a textbook
  layout. The basin is the closest thing to a keypoint dam the site supports, now as recharge, not storage.
- Desert adaptation: rain comes as a few big storms on sand, so the goal shifts from "fill dams" to "slow, spread
  and bank water in the soil and in off-channel storage, and survive the flood." KPC pays only where cover and soil
  hold the moisture (rain-window cover crops, alleys).
- The north lane runs downhill at ~4.9% and doubles as a spillway; treat it as a harvesting surface feeding basins.
- Consider a Regrarians "Book to Meet" or REX Online to have the plan reviewed against the platform.
