# Changes to the current farm plans

Prepared 2026-10-11 by applying this library to the owner's plans (Sep 27 Keyline Plant Plan and V7, Master
Guide, Action Plan and Packet, Pond Design Sheet, keyline_design_v3 map). Ranked by impact. Regulatory facts are
from public sources found in October 2026; confirm them with the agency before spending money.

## Tier 1: change before building or spending

### 1. Test the well before scaling anything; treat hauled water as the base case
- Run a drawdown and recovery test (at least 4 hours pumping, 4 hours recovery, measured flow). Get the DWR Well
  Completion Report and a water quality test (TDS, nitrate, arsenic, fluoride).
- Until then, cost mature demand (~211-231k gal/yr) as hauled at $60/1,000 gal: about $12.7-13.9k/yr.
- A well yielding only 1-3 GPM still works as "trickle and store": a slow pump into a tank on a timer. 1 GPM for
  8 hours a day is about 175k gal/yr.
- Why: a 7 GPM pump over-pumps the existing well and neighbours have drilled far deeper, which suggests a falling
  water table. Minimal Producer status only helps if the well yields.
- Cost: pump test roughly $500-1,500. Deepening or redrilling is a five-figure decision; wait for the test. A new
  well needs an MWA Notice of Intent 15 days before drilling.

### 2. Pond: recharge basin plus covered tanks, not a storage pond
- Leave the basin unlined as an infiltration and recharge basin, sized for the big storms (8.44 in in 22 hours), so
  water soaks into the profile under the downslope tree rows. Plant the edges with natives and willow cuttings;
  rock-armor the spillways.
- Store water for later pumping in covered tanks (e.g. two or three 10-20k gal poly or steel tanks fed by roof and
  pad runoff), about $0.80-1.50 per gallon installed.
- Keep any open water small and shaded (a dish or trough for bees and birds).
- Why: the plan's own numbers show ~780 m³/yr evaporation from a 376 m³ pond. Lancaster: store water in the soil
  first. Keyline dams assume reliable rain high in the landscape.

### 3. Fewer swales; keyline cultivation, tree basins and armored spillways instead
- Keep the cutoff swale above the building pad and one on-contour keyline row as the main water line.
- Replace the other swales with: KPC (a subsoiler pass on the keyline pattern once or twice just before winter
  rains); half-moon or basin microcatchments at each tree sized with C:CA = (CWR − DR) / (DR × K × Eff); rock-lined
  spillways on every basin and berm.
- Run the C:CA check per tree type before digging. Jujube or pomegranate at ~500 mm need, ~250 mm rain, K ≈ 0.4,
  Eff ≈ 0.6 gives a catchment about 4x the basin area; in a dry year it is far larger. Closer rows need drip.
- Why: Doherty estimates KPC at ~$0.02/m² vs ~$0.75/m² for swales; long swales in sand either infiltrate at the top
  or concentrate flow and breach. A rented single-shank ripper covers 1.8 ha in a day.

### 4. Drop or defer aquaponics
- Out for the first 3-5 years; later only as an education demo. High energy and water-treatment demand, hot tanks,
  CDFW aquaculture registration, most tilapia restricted, and Shepard warns about "fish and basil" profit schemes.

## Tier 2: redesign specific enterprises

### 5. Pigs: deep-litter KNF pen, heat plan, county limit
- No more than 9 at a time: two batches of 8-9 finishing spring and fall to miss July-August heat (16-18/yr, not
  the plan's 24).
- Pigs count toward the RL limit only as a primary use; confirm with County Land Use Services before buying stock.
  Pens 70 ft from neighbours' homes and 100 ft from any domestic well.
- Pen: KNF inoculated deep litter (CTAHR LM-23), ~90 cm of wood chips and sawdust with IMO, fully shaded. Controls
  smell and flies, no washing out, makes compost for the tree rows.
- Heat: shade cloth, wallow or mister, double water. Pigs over 100 lb are in danger above 80 °F.
- Sell live-animal shares, priced per head or live weight, never carcass weight.

### 6. Income lines to cover household bills
Keep primary production as the demonstration site; add in this order:
1. Desert-adapted nursery (jujube, pomegranate, fig, mulberry, grape cuttings, mesquite, desert willow, native
   shrubs) grown STUN-style. Needs a CDFA nursery licence and county inspection; starts on the existing nursery pad.
2. Water-harvesting and keyline design-install services for High Desert lots; the farm is the portfolio.
3. Education: workshops, tours, online content on desert keyline and KNF.
4. Value-added: dried jujube, pomegranate molasses, dried tea blends, jam (cottage food Class A). Elderberry syrup
   only if it is on the CDPH approved list.

### 7. Wash margins: vertical mulch and protected transplants instead of seed balls
- Stand dead branches and brush upright (vertical mulch); build Zeedyk one-rock dams and media lunas in the wash;
  plant nursery-grown natives in shelters; seed balls only as a cheap extra before winter rain.
- Check whether a CDFW §1602 agreement applies before working in the channel.
- Why: Arizona seed-ball trials germinated under 10%; a UNLV Mojave study found seeding alone often failed while
  combined treatments and vertical mulch lasted.

### 8. Fruit: lean toward the best water return
- Main crops: jujube (Li, Lang, Sugar Cane, Honey Jar), pomegranate (Wonderful, Parfianka, Kashmir Blend),
  pistachio (with a male pollinizer), wine and table grapes on the north-fence trellis.
- Stone fruit (apricot, peach, plum) at home scale near the house where greywater and frost protection reach.
- Keep the asparagus bed.
- Why: late spring frosts at 4,300 ft hit early bloomers; jujube and pomegranate return the most per gallon.

### 9. Bees: grow to 18 hives only if forage and water support them
- Start with 4-6 hives; register yearly with the county agricultural commissioner.
- Shaded, reliable water; plan for Africanized genetics (requeen, site away from people).
- Plant drought-blooming forage: desert willow, mesquite, rosemary, sages, globemallow.

## Tier 3: cheap additions that compound

10. **Apply for EQIP before planting windbreaks and drip** (380, 612, 441, 311, 325) at beginning-farmer rates.
    Nothing installed before the contract is signed qualifies. See `compliance.md`.
11. **Laundry-to-landscape greywater** to mulch basins at the zone 1 trees: ~10-15k gal/yr, worth ~$600-900/yr at
    hauled prices, ~$200-400 in parts, no permit if it meets CPC Chapter 15.
12. **Mulch and windbreak first, KNF second.** Free wood chips (ChipDrop, tree crews); KNF inputs (IMO, FPJ 1:500,
    LAB 1:1,000) only where water goes; run a compost vs IMO side-by-side on two rows.
13. **Manufactured home:** finish the HCD 433A permanent-foundation step before applying for FSA or other
    land-backed financing.

## Suggested order of work
1. Write the holistic context. Well test, completion report, water quality test.
2. Apply to EQIP. Ask the county about the hog primary-use rule.
3. Build the cutoff swale and the recharge basin with armored spillways. Keyline subsoiling pass before winter rain.
4. Plant windbreaks and the first jujube and pomegranate rows with basins and drip. Set up greywater.
5. Nursery licence; start propagation on the nursery pad.
6. Deep-litter pig pen and a first batch of no more than 9 in spring. Start 4-6 hives.
7. Add value-added products and services as the site matures into a demonstration.
