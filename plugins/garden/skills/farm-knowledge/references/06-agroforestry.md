# Agroforestry

## The five USDA practices (National Agroforestry Center)
| Practice | What it is | NRCS standard | Use here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windbreaks / shelterbelts | Tree and shrub rows across damaging wind | 380 (650 renovation) | Top priority: S/SW and W bands; consider NE for Santa Anas |
| Alley cropping | Tree rows with crops or forage between | 311 | Keyline rows with cover crop, asparagus, melons, grazing |
| Silvopasture | Trees, forage and livestock together | 381 | Pig and poultry rotation under mesquite and pistachio rows |
| Riparian forest buffers | Trees and shrubs along waterways | 391 | Wash margin: desert willow, mesquite, rabbitbrush, outside the 12 m flood buffer |
| Forest farming | Crops under existing canopy | 379 | Not applicable |

Related standards: 422 Hedgerow, 612 Tree/Shrub Establishment, 484 Mulching, 441 Micro-irrigation, 603 Herbaceous
Wind Barrier, 327 Conservation Cover (pollinator), 386 Field Border, 342 Critical Area Planting.

## Windbreak design rules (NAC / NRCS)
- Protection reaches about **2-5 H upwind and 10-15 H downwind** (H = mature height); greatest reduction at 4-6 H.
- **Density 40-60%** for crops; 60-80% for livestock and buildings. Solid barriers make turbulence.
- Perpendicular to the most damaging winds (here S-SW, plus fall NE Santa Anas).
- Continuous: gaps funnel wind; gap only where needed for the wash or cold-air drainage.
- Length at least 10x height.
- Rows: tall evergreen (Afghan/Eldarica pine, Arizona cypress, juniper) + medium (pinyon, mesquite) + shrub
  (fourwing saltbush, Apache plume, elderberry, sumac).
- Drip for 3-5 years; weed-free strip; rodent protection.

## Alley cropping and silvopasture
- Alley width = a multiple of the widest implement or fencing cell. Keyline rows trump E-W orientation when water
  drives the layout.
- Tree row: two staggered lines ~3-3.5 m apart with understory shrubs.
- Animals: guard young trees (guards, electric offset wire); pigs root, so use rings or short stays near trees;
  poultry are the safest early integrator.

## Food forests and syntropic agroforestry
- Food forest (Hart, Crawford, Jacke): seven layers plus fungi.
- Syntropic farming (Ernst Götsch): very dense planting by stratum and succession with heavy chop-and-drop pruning.
  Brilliant where water is not limiting; in drylands it needs drip and very heavy mulch.
- Rule here: syntropic or food-forest density only in irrigated, sheltered zones (house garden, greenhouse
  surroundings, nursery edge, greywater outlets). Keep the field as wider-spaced savanna rows.

## Dryland establishment
- **Nurse plants:** seedlings survive far better under a nurse shrub or shade (shade cloth, tree shelters, a rock on
  the SW side).
- **Deep watering** to train roots down; move emitters outward as the tree grows; perforated deep pipes.
- **Mulch:** 4-6 in wood chips off the trunk; rock mulch where wind scours.
- **Trunk whitewash** (diluted white latex) against sunscald and to delay bloom.
- **Frost drainage:** leave gaps for cold air to flow downslope (the plan has an 8 m gap).
- **Rodents and rabbits:** jackrabbits and gophers are major killers; hardware-cloth baskets and guards.
