# Holistic management, livestock and bees

## Holistic Management (Allan Savory)
- *Holistic Management* (3rd ed., Savory & Butterfield, 2016); Savory Institute; Holistic Management International.
- **Holistic context:** the whole under management (decision makers, resource base, money), a quality-of-life
  statement, and the future resource base. Here: self-sufficiency, farm covers household bills, 4-16 hours a week.
- **Testing questions** for every decision: cause and effect; weak link (social, biological, financial); marginal
  reaction (which next dollar or hour gives the most toward the goal); gross profit analysis; energy and money
  source and use; sustainability; society and culture.
- **Financial planning:** plan profit first. Income − Profit = Expenses. Wealth-generating expenses get priority.
- **Brittleness:** seasonally dry environments degrade under rest and need disturbance to cycle dead material. The
  Mojave is very brittle.
- **Evidence:** Hawkins (2017) meta-analysis found no advantage of holistic planned grazing over continuous grazing
  for plant cover or biomass; Savory-affiliated rebuttals dispute it. Treat the decision framework as robust and
  grazing-carbon claims as unproven.

## Pigs on a desert smallholding
- **Heat:** pigs cannot sweat. Above ~85 °F they need shade, a wallow or misters and constant water (growers drink
  ~2-5 gal/day, more in heat). Utah State Extension: pigs over ~100 lb can slide into life-threatening heat stress
  above 80 °F even in dry air. Double the water, shade or bury water lines (pigs won't drink hot water), handle only
  early or late. In winter, cold pigs may need up to 3x feed; dry bedding, draft-free but ventilated housing.
- **Timing:** batches finishing spring and fall avoid July-August heat (the owner's plan: up to 9 at a time, two
  batches a year).
- **Feed is the cost driver.** Offsets: alley forage in the rain window, fruit drops, whey, spent brewers' grain,
  bakery and produce waste (California rules: no meat; cooked garbage feeding is regulated, check CDFA), sprouted
  fodder, mesquite pods, prickly pear pads.
- **Breeds:** grazing types (Kunekune, American Guinea Hog, Idaho Pasture Pig) eat more forage and grow slower;
  hardy pasture breeds (Berkshire, Red Wattle, Large Black, Hereford, Duroc crosses) grow faster. Dark skin handles
  sun better.
- **Rooting:** nose rings on cover, or use rooting deliberately to prepare beds; limit time per cell on fragile crust.
- **KNF deep litter** (CTAHR LM-23) for the base pen (`04-knf-jadam.md`).
- County limits and how to sell pork: `compliance.md`.

## Poultry
- Layers in a mobile coop following pigs or rotating through alleys; KNF deep-litter coop as the base. An existing
  coop holds 14 hens and a rooster (plan: eggs ~$1,000-1,500/yr).
- Broilers in Salatin pens are cheap in water and feed per pound and cash-flow fast; on-farm processing up to 1,000
  birds/yr falls under the federal producer/grower exemption, but check California rules with CDFA before selling.
- Ducks are a poor fit unless water is free.
- Egg sales rules: `compliance.md`.

## Bees
- Annual apiary registration with the county agricultural commissioner. Southern California bees are largely
  Africanized or hybrid: requeen with gentle stock, site hives away from people and animals, keep a water source so
  bees don't visit neighbours' pools.
- **Forage** is pulse-based: spring wildflowers, mesquite May-Jun, desert willow and rabbitbrush late. The Jul-Sep
  dearth needs planted forage or feeding. The plan cut the apiary from 30 to 18 hives; start with 4-6.
- **Income:** honey, nucs and queens (higher margin, less forage-dependent), pollination contracts (almonds in
  February), wax, propolis, classes.
- **Varroa** decides survival: monitor with alcohol washes; oxalic acid when broodless; formic or thymol by temperature.
- Resources: UC Davis Honey and Pollination Center; Sammataro *The Beekeeper's Handbook*; Randy Oliver's
  ScientificBeekeeping.com.

## Sheep and goats
- Sheep fit tree alleys better than goats (goats strip bark). Hair sheep (Dorper, Katahdin, St. Croix) handle heat
  and need no shearing.
- On 5 desert acres there is little standing forage; small ruminants are fed animals that give impact and manure.
