# Masanobu Fukuoka and natural farming

## Who and books
Masanobu Fukuoka (1913-2008), Japanese plant pathologist who farmed rice, winter grain and citrus on Shikoku for ~60
years without tillage or chemicals. *The One-Straw Revolution* (1975; English 1978); *The Natural Way of Farming*
(1985, technical); *The Road Back to Nature* (1987); *Sowing Seeds in the Desert* (2012, ed. Larry Korn, his
desert seed-ball work); Larry Korn, *One-Straw Revolutionary* (2015).

## Four principles
1. No cultivation.
2. No chemical fertiliser or prepared compost (no imported fertiliser; he did return his own straw).
3. No weeding by tillage or herbicides (straw, clover and timing instead).
4. No dependence on chemicals.

## Practice
- Rice-barley-clover rotation: broadcast clover and winter grain into standing rice; return all straw uncut; sow rice
  in clay seed balls into standing barley.
- **Seed balls (nendo dango):** seed in clay (sometimes compost) pellets broadcast on the surface, protected from
  birds, insects and drying until rain.
- Orchard: citrus with acacia (N-fixing windbreak), clover groundcover, semi-wild vegetables, poultry.
- "How about not doing this?" before every intervention.

## Desert work and what the evidence says
- He advocated broadcasting seed balls of 100+ species before rain so each micro-site selects its survivors.
  Follower results (India, Greece, Kenya) are mixed and anecdotal.
- **University of Arizona AZ1937 (2021):** seed balls (1 seed : 3 nutrient : 5 clay); manure base best, soil worst;
  ball size made no difference; larger seeds did better; germination under 10% overall.
- **Seed-pellet reviews (Montana State 2025; U. Arizona):** pellets can protect from granivores and crusting, but
  results vary by species, recipe and the rain that follows.
- **UNLV for Clark County (Chiquoine & Abella, 2025),** 15 Mojave restoration projects up to 22 years old: seeding
  alone often showed no benefit after 16 years. Combined treatments (topsoil + planting, ripping + planting) and
  vertical mulch (dead shrubs stood upright) lasted. Test small, phase inputs, protect from herbivores, monitor.
- **BLM Mojave Native Plant Program:** limits are seed predators, invasive annual grasses and rain timing. Priority
  species: desert globemallow, Nevada jointfir, burrobrush, creosote, Indian ricegrass, big galleta. Source seed
  from the local seed zone.

Seed balls are a cheap, low-odds lottery. Put the effort into vertical mulch, rock mulch, protected transplants and
basins.

## How it applies here
- Seed-ball the wash margins, windbreak gaps and disturbed ground in Oct-Nov just before winter rain: native
  bunchgrasses, globemallow, desert marigold, lupine, phacelia, rabbitbrush, fourwing saltbush, desert willow,
  scarified mesquite, plus winter cover (fava, oats, Austrian pea). Manure-based balls, larger seeds, paired with
  vertical and rock mulch.
- No-till alleys: broadcast the winter cover into stubble or mown residue; leave residue as summer mulch.
- "How about not doing this?" fits a water-limited site: every pass, irrigation and input costs water and money.
