# West Cajon farm brief

A condensed, public-safe summary of the farm around this garden, for the garden chat page.
The full version is the plugin's farm-knowledge skill (`plugins/garden/skills/farm-knowledge`).
Keep personal details (address, parcel numbers, finances, contracts) out of this file; the repo is public.

## Site
- 5 acres in Phelan, CA, about 4,330-4,360 ft. Mojave / high-desert transition (Joshua tree, juniper, creosote scrub). Alkaline sandy alluvium assumed until a soil test says otherwise. Fenced field about 181 x 100 m. A wash draining ~50 ha crosses the property.
- The station's first year (Oct 2025-Sep 2026) was a wet El Nino year: 24 in of rain, ~21 in of it Nov-Feb, one storm of 8.4 in in 22 hours. Long-term normals are far drier (roughly 6-14 in, unverified), so storage is sized for a dry year of 6-8 in.
- Winds: S/SW gusts most of the year, NE Santa Anas in fall.
- Water is hauled at $60 per 1,000 gal. The existing well is weak and its yield has never been tested. Mature farm demand in the plan is ~211-231k gal/yr (about $12.7-13.9k/yr if hauled).

## Owner's decisions (Oct 2026; these override older plan documents)
1. Goal: self-sufficiency. The farm feeds the household first (groceries not bought count as income) and aims to cover the household's current bills.
2. Time: 4-16 hours a week beside a day job. Automate first (drip on timers, deep mulch, deep-litter pens). Start with low-hours income: eggs, pig shares, nursery stock from cuttings.
3. Pond: an unlined regeneration / recharge basin that fills in storms and soaks in. Water for later pumping goes in covered tanks.
4. Pigs: at most 9 at a time, two batches a year finishing spring and fall (16-18 pigs/yr) on KNF deep litter.

## Design rules
1. Plan in Regrarians order: climate, geography, water, access, ecosystems, buildings, fences, soils, economy, energy.
2. Water is the binding constraint and arrives in pulses: design for the 8-inch storm and the six dry months at once. Plan every overflow as a resource.
3. Rank enterprises by net $ per 1,000 gal and per labour hour. Under ~$60 per 1,000 gal loses money on hauled water. Plan figures: pork ~$621, nursery ~$393, jujube ~$53, pomegranate ~$50, stone fruit $9-13 per 1,000 gal.
4. Desert-adapted keyline: few swales; keyline pattern cultivation (a subsoiler pass before winter rain), many small tree basins and half-moons sized with C:CA = (CWR - DR) / (DR x K x Eff), rock-armored spillways, off-channel storage. Passive treatments (cover, litter, animal impact) before earthworks.
5. Open water evaporates (~2 m/yr). Store water in soil and covered tanks; ponds are for recharge.
6. Biome model: desert wash / mesquite bosque with a pinyon-juniper edge (honey mesquite, desert willow, blue elderberry, saltbush, Apache plume, pinyon, juniper). Main crops by water return: jujube, pomegranate, pistachio, grape, fig. Stone fruit at home scale only. Grow mass plantings from seed and cuttings to find adapted genetics.
7. Windbreaks (S/SW and W, consider NE for Santa Anas), shade and wood-chip mulch come before any soil input.
8. Soil biology needs moisture: KNF/JADAM inputs only where water already goes (basins, drip zones, compost, deep-litter pens).
9. Revegetating the wash: vertical mulch, one-rock dams, media lunas and protected transplants first; seed balls only as a cheap extra before winter rain.
10. Income realism: primary production nets about $17.6-31.6k/yr at maturity, so cash also has to come from nursery stock, water-harvesting design-install work, education and value-added products, with the farm as the demonstration.
Two tests for any new project: does it cut a household cost or bring in more per 1,000 gal and per hour than what it replaces? Does it fit the 4-16 hour week?

## Recommended plan changes (ranked)
1. Test the well first: a drawdown and recovery test (4 h pumping, 4 h recovery, roughly $500-1,500), the DWR well completion report and a water quality test. Plan on hauled water until then. Even 1 GPM into a tank on a timer is ~175k gal/yr.
2. Pond as an unlined recharge basin sized for the big storms, edges planted with natives and willow cuttings, spillways rock-armored. Two or three 10-20k gal covered tanks fed by roof and pad runoff (~$0.80-1.50/gal installed).
3. Keep the cutoff swale above the building pad and one keyline row; replace other swales with keyline cultivation, tree basins and armored spillways. Jujube or pomegranate needs a catchment about 4x the basin area in an average year, far more in a dry one; closer rows need drip.
4. Defer aquaponics 3-5 years (energy, water treatment, CDFW permits).
5. Pigs: shaded KNF deep-litter pen (~90 cm wood chips and sawdust with IMO), batches timed to miss July-August heat; pigs over 100 lb are in danger above 80 F. Sell live-animal shares priced per head or live weight.
6. Income lines in order: desert-adapted nursery; water-harvesting and keyline design-install services for High Desert lots; workshops and tours; value-added (dried jujube, pomegranate molasses, tea blends, jam).
7. Fruit: jujube (Li, Lang, Sugar Cane, Honey Jar), pomegranate (Wonderful, Parfianka, Kashmir Blend), pistachio with a male pollinizer, grapes on the north-fence trellis; stone fruit near the house. Keep the asparagus bed.
8. Bees: start with 4-6 hives, shaded water, drought-blooming forage (desert willow, mesquite, rosemary, sages, globemallow); plan for Africanized genetics.
9. Apply for NRCS EQIP before planting windbreaks and drip; nothing installed before the contract is signed is paid.
10. Laundry-to-landscape greywater to zone 1 tree basins: ~10-15k gal/yr for ~$200-400 in parts.

Order of work: holistic context and well test; EQIP application and county hog question; cutoff swale, recharge basin and keyline subsoiling before winter rain; windbreaks and first jujube and pomegranate rows with basins and drip, plus greywater; nursery licence and propagation; deep-litter pig pen (first batch of up to 9 in spring) and 4-6 hives; value-added products and services.

## Rules and permits (researched Oct 2026; confirm with the agency before spending)
- Groundwater: Mojave Water Agency adjudicated basin. Under 10 acre-feet/yr is a Minimal Producer (fee on the tax bill). A new well needs an MWA Notice of Intent 15 days before drilling.
- County RL zone: hogs 1 per 20,000 sq ft, max 9, as a primary use (confirm with Land Use Services); roosters need 1 acre, max 9; pens 70 ft from neighbours' homes and 100 ft from domestic wells.
- Meat: live-animal shares under custom-exempt rules; retail cuts need USDA-inspected slaughter.
- Eggs: CDFA egg handler registration ($75, then $50/yr), keep at 45 F or colder, cage-free required. A farm stand under a county Community Food Producer registration allows up to 15 dozen a month.
- Cottage food Class A covers jams, dried fruit and dried tea blends. Nursery sales need a CDFA nursery licence, county inspection and seller's permit. Bees need yearly county apiary registration.
- Earthworks need a county grading permit; work in the wash may need a CDFW 1602 agreement. Rooftop rainwater needs no water right.
- Funding: NRCS EQIP (beginning farmer rates, advance payment, apply before the mid-January cutoff), USDA FSA microloans, Mojave Desert RCD, Western SARE grants, CDFA Healthy Soils (producer funding expected 2027).
